Nonna's Classic Tomato Pasta

Serves 2 | Prep time: 10 minutes | Cook time: 30 minutes | Total time: 40 minutes

Ingredients

For the tomato sauce

  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 small onion, finely diced
  • 1 x 400 g can whole peeled tomatoes
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 4–6 fresh basil leaves
  • A tiny pinch of sugar, only if the tomatoes are very acidic
  • Optional: 1/2 teaspoon chilli flakes

For the pasta

  • 200 g dried pasta
  • Plenty of water for boiling
  • Salt for the pasta water
  • 1/2–1 cup reserved pasta water
  • 25–40 g freshly grated Parmesan or Pecorino, optional
  • Fresh basil, to finish
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, to finish

Before You Begin

Listen, my darling: tomato pasta is simple food, but simple food must be treated properly. You do not need twenty ingredients. You need good tomatoes, good olive oil, garlic, basil, pasta, and a little patience.

Take your tomatoes and crush them with your hands before cooking, or crush them directly in the pan with the back of your wooden spoon. Do not worry if they are not perfectly smooth. A little texture is beautiful.

And please, do not rush the sauce. Let the tomatoes cook gently until they become sweet, rich, and concentrated. The pasta will be ready when the sauce is ready.

Step 1: Prepare the Sauce

Place a wide frying pan or saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the olive oil and allow it to warm gently. The oil should be warm and fluid, but it should not be smoking.

Add the finely diced onion and cook for approximately 4–5 minutes. Stir occasionally and let the onion become soft and translucent. We are looking for sweetness, not browning.

Now add the sliced garlic. If you are using chilli flakes, add them at the same time. Stir continuously for about 30–60 seconds, just until the garlic becomes fragrant.

Be careful here. Garlic burns quickly, and burnt garlic will make your beautiful sauce bitter. If the garlic starts turning dark brown, you have gone too far, my dear.

Step 2: Add the Tomatoes

Add the tomato paste and cook it with the onion and garlic for about 1 minute. Stir constantly. Cooking the tomato paste briefly gives the sauce a deeper and more developed flavour.

Add the crushed tomatoes to the pan. Add the salt, black pepper, and dried oregano. Stir everything together with your wooden spoon.

Bring the sauce to a gentle simmer. Once you see small bubbles forming, reduce the heat to low.

Leave the pan uncovered and allow the sauce to simmer gently for 20–25 minutes. Stir every few minutes so nothing catches on the bottom.

The sauce should slowly become thicker and darker. The sharp smell of raw tomatoes should disappear, replaced by a sweet, rich tomato aroma.

Nonna's Rule

Do not add sugar before tasting the sauce. Good tomatoes are already naturally sweet. If your tomatoes taste particularly acidic after simmering, add only a tiny pinch of sugar and cook for another minute.

Step 3: Cook the Pasta

While the tomato sauce is simmering, bring a large pot of water to a vigorous boil.

Salt the water generously once it is boiling. The pasta water should taste pleasantly seasoned. This is one of the few opportunities you have to season the pasta itself, so do not be shy.

Add the pasta to the boiling water and stir immediately. This prevents the pieces from sticking together.

Cook the pasta according to the package instructions, but start tasting it approximately 2 minutes before the recommended cooking time.

You want the pasta al dente. It should be cooked through but still have a gentle firmness when you bite it.

Do not cook it until it is completely soft. The pasta will continue cooking when you finish it in the tomato sauce.

Step 4: Save the Pasta Water

Before draining the pasta, remove at least 1/2 cup of the hot pasta water. You can take more if you like. Nonna always says it is better to have it and not need it than to need it and have already thrown it away.

Drain the pasta, but do not rinse it.

Rinsing removes the starch from the surface of the pasta. That starch is exactly what helps the tomato sauce cling beautifully to every piece.

Step 5: Finish the Pasta in the Sauce

Add the drained pasta directly into the pan containing the tomato sauce.

Increase the heat slightly to medium-low and toss the pasta with the sauce. Add approximately 2 tablespoons of reserved pasta water and continue tossing.

Keep tossing for 1–2 minutes. The pasta should absorb some of the sauce while releasing a little starch into it.

If the sauce looks too thick, add another splash of pasta water. Do not pour in a large amount all at once. Add it gradually until the sauce becomes smooth and glossy.

If the sauce looks watery, simply continue cooking and tossing for another minute. The excess liquid will evaporate.

This is the moment when the pasta and sauce become one dish. You should not have pasta sitting underneath a puddle of tomato sauce. The sauce should coat the pasta.

Step 6: Add the Basil

Turn off the heat.

Tear the fresh basil leaves with your hands and add them to the pasta. Give everything one final toss.

If you are using Parmesan or Pecorino, add a small amount now and toss until incorporated.

Taste the pasta.

Does it need more salt? Add a little. Does it need more pepper? Add it. Is the sauce too thick? Add another tiny splash of pasta water.

Do not be afraid to taste your food. This is how you learn to cook.

Step 7: Serve

Warm your plates or bowls if you can. Use tongs to lift and twist the pasta onto each plate.

Finish with a few fresh basil leaves, freshly grated Parmesan or Pecorino if desired, a little black pepper, and a very small drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil.

Serve immediately while the pasta is hot.

How You Know It Is Perfect

The tomato sauce should be rich but not heavy. It should taste distinctly of tomato, with the garlic and basil supporting it rather than overpowering it.

The pasta should be al dente, never mushy.

The sauce should cling to the pasta rather than collect at the bottom of the bowl.

The finished pasta should look glossy. That glossy finish comes from properly combining the pasta, sauce, olive oil, and starchy pasta water.

Nonna's Most Important Tips

Do not burn the garlic. Garlic needs only a short time in warm oil. Keep the heat moderate and keep stirring.

Do not skip seasoning. Salt the tomato sauce gradually and salt the pasta water properly.

Do not rinse the pasta. The starch is your friend.

Do not drown the pasta. You want enough sauce to coat every piece, not enough to make tomato soup.

Do not throw away all the pasta water. Save some before draining.

Do not overcook the pasta. It will continue cooking when you toss it with the hot sauce.

Do not bury everything under cheese. Cheese should complement the tomato sauce, not hide it.

Optional Variations

Spicy Tomato Pasta: Add 1/2–1 teaspoon chilli flakes to the olive oil together with the garlic. Let the chilli gently infuse the oil before adding the tomatoes.

Creamy Tomato Pasta: Once the pasta is finished, turn off the heat and stir in 2–3 tablespoons of cream. Toss until smooth and glossy.

Tomato and Mozzarella Pasta: Finish the pasta with small pieces of fresh mozzarella and fresh basil. The residual heat will soften the mozzarella without completely melting it.

Vegetable Tomato Pasta: Add mushrooms, zucchini, roasted peppers, spinach, or aubergine to the sauce for a more substantial meal.

Extra-Garlic Pasta: Add one additional garlic clove, but remember that more garlic does not mean better garlic. Cook it gently.

Storage

Leftover tomato pasta can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.

For the best texture, reheat it gently in a frying pan rather than putting it straight into a microwave for several minutes.

Add a small splash of water while reheating and toss the pasta until the sauce becomes glossy again.

Nonna's Final Word

Now you sit down and eat.

Not twenty minutes later. Not after taking fifty photographs. Immediately.

Simple tomato pasta is one of those dishes where the quality comes from doing the little things properly: gentle heat, good tomatoes, properly salted pasta water, al dente pasta, and finishing everything together in the pan.

And if there is some sauce left on the plate when the pasta is finished, you take a piece of bread and mop it up.

Of course you do.

That is not wasteful.

That is buon appetito.
